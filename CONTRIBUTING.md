# Contributing

Thank you for considering a contribution. This list covers **enterprise and operational risk management method**: gap analysis, qualitative and quantitative risk analysis, heat maps, RACI matrices, the risk register, compensating controls, continuous monitoring, and periodic review cadence — plus the standards (ISO 31000, COSO ERM, NIST SP 800-30/39, FAIR) that formalise it.

## What belongs here

- Official standards and their supporting documents (ISO 31000/31010, COSO ERM, NIST SP 800-30/39/137/161, FAIR).
- Practical, named risk-analysis techniques (FMEA, bow-tie, Delphi, Monte Carlo, STRIDE) with a credible source.
- Templates and worksheets that help a practitioner start a risk register, RACI matrix, heat map, or compensating-control justification rather than build one from scratch.
- Commercial GRC platforms with a named, risk-register-specific feature set (not just a general compliance-automation landing page).
- Certification and training paths specific to risk management (CRISC, Open FAIR, PMI-RMP, GERM).
- Government and standards bodies that own or maintain the frameworks referenced here.

## What does not belong here

- Framework-specific control catalogues (NIST CSF, ISO 27001, PCI-DSS, CIS Controls) — covered by the companion [Security Frameworks](https://github.com/garynair/security-frameworks) list.
- Sector-specific regulatory risk obligations (GLBA, NYDFS 500, HIPAA, FedRAMP) — covered by the companion sector lists (see Related Lists in the README).
- Generic project-management content without a risk-specific angle.
- Vendor marketing content without a substantive free tier, open-source component, or named feature.
- Self-promotion of products you have not used or do not work with.

## How to submit

1. Open an issue titled `[Submission]: Resource Name`, or open a PR directly.
2. Add the entry in alphabetical order within the relevant section, in the format: `- [Name](URL) - one sentence on what it is, one sentence on why a practitioner would care.`
3. If proposing a new template, include a filled-in example, not just an empty structure.
4. No marketing language, no unverified performance claims.
5. If you are the author or maintainer of the resource, disclose that in the issue or PR description.

## Style

- British English in the description copy.
- No emojis.
- Avoid banned vocabulary (delve, leverage, harness, robust, seamless, holistic, transformative, paradigm). Plain language wins.
- Flag paywalled standards explicitly (as the ISO 31000/31010 and COSO entries do) rather than implying free access.

## Quality bar

- Must be publicly accessible (open-source, free tier, or the standard's official paywalled source), or a mature commercial platform with a named feature set.
- Must be actively maintained (commit within 12 months, or a standards body with an active update cycle).
- Description must be factual.

## Review

A maintainer reviews PRs within seven days. Most PRs that meet the criteria above land within two weeks.
